A 61-year-old man presented to the emergency department.

Past history: type 2 diabetes, prior stroke, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and a known thyroid nodule and goiter. Home medications include clopidogrel and apixaban.

History of present illness: For the last 5 days he has had black stools and fever, which progressed to profuse diarrhea, fever, and chills. He also reports intermittent sharp lower abdominal pain. He denies nausea and vomiting. He denies sick contacts and recent tick bites. Four days earlier he had visited the emergency department with similar symptoms; labs then showed mild hyponatremia (sodium 130 mEq/L) and he was discharged.

Examination: He was confused on presentation. He had orthostatic hypotension.

Admission laboratory results:
- Sodium 126 mEq/L
- Potassium 4.0 mEq/L
- Creatinine 0.76 mg/dL
- BUN 7 mg/dL
- Glucose 165 mg/dL
- Calcium 8.2 mg/dL
- Albumin 3.1 g/dL
- AST 140 U/L
- ALT 73 U/L
- WBC 4.4 x10^3/uL
- TSH 0.25 uIU/mL

Imaging: Head CT on admission showed no acute changes; stable areas of encephalomalacia in the left frontal and parietal lobes consistent with the prior stroke.
