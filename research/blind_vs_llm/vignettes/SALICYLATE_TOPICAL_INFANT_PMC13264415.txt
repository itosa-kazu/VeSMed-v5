A 6-month-old girl (weight 4.56 kg) was brought to the emergency department.

Background: No significant past medical history; unvaccinated and without routine pediatric care. Since 3 months of age she has had a persistent, pruritic, dry rash that started on the face and spread caudally. At home, caregivers have been applying an over-the-counter product containing 2% salicylic acid multiple times daily for several months (most recent application the morning of presentation), as well as calendula oil/vitamin E. They also give oral herbal teething drops containing clove and spearmint oils.

History of present illness: Over the several days before presentation she developed poor feeding/decreased oral intake and progressive lethargy. On the day of presentation she had increased irritability and vomiting after the routine application of the topical products.

Examination: Afebrile but ill-appearing and listless (altered mental status). Clinically dehydrated with dry mucous membranes and sunken eyes. Heart rate 204 beats/min. Blood pressure 69/25 mmHg (mean arterial pressure about 40 mmHg). Diffuse erythematous, dry, scaly rash without vesicles or pustules.

Initial results:
- Venous blood gas: pH 6.98, bicarbonate 5.7 mmol/L, pCO2 22 mmHg
- Lactate 12.15 mmol/L
- Potassium 6.2 mmol/L, with peaked T waves on cardiac monitoring
- Albumin 2.6 g/dL
- PT 86.8 s, INR >9.36

Imaging: Head CT unremarkable.
