A 25-year-old woman with a history of mood disorder, treated as an outpatient with venlafaxine, mirtazapine and lamotrigine, was found unconscious in her apartment by a friend. An empty package labeled as containing 5 g of potassium cyanide and a suicide note were found next to her; the product and amount were not verified. Several antidepressant tablets were missing from their packages. She was brought to hospital by emergency services.

On admission:
- Unconscious, Glasgow Coma Scale 6
- Inadequate spontaneous respiration; breathing shallow and irregular
- Trismus
- Oxygen saturation about 92%; blood pressure 75/45 mmHg; heart rate 120/min (circulatory failure)
- Pupils initially dilated, later mid-sized, with anisocoria (right > left)
- Skin cool and moist, with preserved pink coloration (no cyanosis)
- No bitter almond odor detected

Arterial blood gas on admission: pH 7.004, HCO3- 8.2 mmol/L, PCO2 34.6 mmHg, PO2 80.7 mmHg, lactate 22.0 mmol/L, base excess -23.0 mmol/L (profound metabolic acidosis).

Laboratory tests on admission (reference ranges in parentheses):
- WBC 28.62 × 10^3/µL (4.23–10.00); hemoglobin 13.4 g/dL (12.0–15.7); RBC 4.31 × 10^6/µL; hematocrit 42.7%; MCV 99.1 fL (79.4–94.8); MCH 31.1 pg; MCHC 31.4 g/dL; platelets 331 × 10^3/µL
- Urea 41.5 mg/dL (17.0–43.0); creatinine 1.72 mg/dL (0.60–0.90); acute kidney injury
- Sodium 142.7 mmol/L; potassium 4.26 mmol/L; magnesium 3.27 mg/dL (1.90–2.50); total calcium 2.40 mmol/L
- AST 52 IU/L (10–31); ALT 18 IU/L (10–31)
- PT 12.9 s; INR 1.16
- Creatine kinase 384 U/L (25–175)
- CRP 34.1 mg/L (< 5.0)
- Glucose 97 mg/dL

Imaging: noncontrast head CT on the first hospital day showed no acute abnormalities; initial chest radiograph showed normal lung aeration with no pulmonary edema or focal inflammatory changes.
