A 68-year-old man with no notable medical history is admitted because of fever for 3 days and limb convulsions with impaired consciousness for 13 hours. Before admission he had a low-grade fever that transiently improved; he then suddenly developed limb convulsions and impaired consciousness. He denies exposure to toxins or harmful substances. No rash has been present.

Examination: He is confused; formal testing of cortical function, orientation, speech, strength, and sensation cannot be completed because of poor cooperation. Pupils are equal, round, and reactive, but the light reflex is sluggish. All four limbs move slightly to painful stimulation. No pathological reflexes bilaterally. Neck stiffness is present (chin-to-chest distance three finger-breadths). Cardiopulmonary and abdominal examinations are unremarkable. (Numeric vital signs not reported.)

Brain MRI: multiple FLAIR-hyperintense lesions in the right temporal lobe, cerebellar vermis, superior parts of both cerebellar hemispheres, cerebral peduncle, and the region adjacent to the posterior horn of the right lateral ventricle; no contrast enhancement.

EEG: mild nonspecific background abnormalities without definite epileptiform discharges.

Lumbar puncture:
- Opening pressure 200 mmH2O (reference 80-180)
- CSF white blood cells 400 cells/uL (reference 0-8)
- CSF protein 2.05 g/L (reference 0.20-0.40)
- CSF glucose 3.9 mmol/L (reference 2.5-4.4)
- CSF chloride 108 mmol/L (reference 120-130)
