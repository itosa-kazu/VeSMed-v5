A 55-year-old woman with known advanced, inoperable high-grade serous ovarian cancer (FIGO stage IIIC, diagnosed at laparoscopy one month earlier) was hospitalized after the first cycle of neoadjuvant chemotherapy with paclitaxel and carboplatin. She presented with acute abdominal pain and bloody diarrhea.

Abdominal CT: ascites with peritoneal deposits, an enlarged right ovary, omental caking, and circumferential bowel wall thickening with marked submucosal edema.

Despite aggressive intravenous fluid therapy she developed oliguria. No vital signs were reported.

Laboratory tests showed acute kidney injury, thrombocytopenia and markedly elevated LDH (reference ranges in parentheses):
- CRP 82.6 mg/L (< 5)
- Leukocytes 18.4 × 10^9/L (4.0–10.0); neutrophils 78%, lymphocytes 5%, monocytes 6%
- Hemoglobin 125 g/L (120–150)
- Platelets 114 × 10^9/L (150–450)
- Creatinine 250 µmol/L (44–80)
- Blood urea nitrogen 16.3 mmol/L (1.7–8.3)
- LDH 18.81 µkat/L (< 4.13)
- AST 0.83 µkat/L (< 0.52); ALT 0.52 µkat/L (< 0.56); GGT 1.00 µkat/L (< 0.63)
- Peripheral blood smear: schistocytes 0.5% (< 0.1%)
- Sodium 127.7 mmol/L; potassium 4.32 mmol/L; chloride 99.9 mmol/L
