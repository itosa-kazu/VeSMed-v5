A 36-year-old woman presented to the hospital.

Background: Chronic leukopenia since 2017 without prior clinical consequences; previous breast reduction surgery. In the days before her symptoms began she had been caring for her husband, who had an upper respiratory tract infection (he had recovered by the time of her admission).

History of present illness: 3 days of fever, chills, diarrhea, and vomiting, with chest pain radiating toward the abdomen, and dyspnea. She denied headache and photophobia.

Examination on arrival: Conscious and oriented (GCS recorded as 13-14 on the admission organ-failure assessment); no signs of meningeal irritation and no focal neurological deficits. Heart rate 124 beats/min, blood pressure 65/44 mmHg (mean arterial pressure about 51 mmHg), oxygen saturation 90% on room air. Lungs clear to auscultation. Diffuse abdominal tenderness. Marked skin and soft-tissue changes over the left thoracic and abdominal wall, including phlegmon and bullae.

Admission laboratory results:
- CRP 453.04 mg/L
- Procalcitonin 19.38 ng/mL
- Urea 10.84 mmol/L (BUN about 30 mg/dL)
- Creatinine 240 umol/L (2.71 mg/dL)
- Creatine kinase 10.34 ukat/L (about 620 U/L)
- WBC 2.39 x10^9/L: neutrophils 74.4%, lymphocytes 9.9%, monocytes 0.8%, eosinophils 1.7%, basophils 0%
- INR 1.44
- Platelets 113 x10^3/uL
- Total bilirubin 9.28 umol/L (0.54 mg/dL)
- PaO2/FiO2 208.5 (not mechanically ventilated)

Imaging on the day of admission:
- Abdominal ultrasound: changes consistent with sigmoid and descending colitis.
- CT chest and abdomen (2 hours after admission): large left pulmonary infiltrate, bilateral pleural effusions, left axillary lymphadenopathy, phlegmonous changes of the left thoracic and abdominal wall, and early ischemic changes in the liver, spleen, and kidneys.
