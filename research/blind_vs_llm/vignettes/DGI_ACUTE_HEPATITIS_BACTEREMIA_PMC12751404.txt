A 31-year-old man presents to the emergency department with a 4-day history of epigastric pain.

History and exposures: He recently returned from an out-of-state vacation within the United States, during which he reported several new sexual contacts. He is not aware of any new food exposures or known sick contacts. Past medical history includes syphilis, chlamydia, and herpes zoster (all adequately treated) and a prior cholecystectomy. He takes emtricitabine/tenofovir disoproxil fumarate as HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis.

Symptoms: Since symptom onset 4 days ago he has had epigastric pain, nausea, fever up to 38.5 °C, penile discharge, and diarrhea. He reports no joint or skin symptoms.

Examination: In the emergency department he is tachycardic, tachypneic, and febrile (numeric vital signs not reported). No arthritis, tenosynovitis, or rash.

Laboratory results at presentation:
- Total bilirubin 7.8 mg/dL; conjugated bilirubin 5.9 mg/dL
- AST 8520 U/L; ALT 5814 U/L; alkaline phosphatase 212 U/L
- Total protein 6.3 g/dL; albumin 3.6 g/dL
- White blood cell count and hemoglobin within normal limits
- Sodium 131 mmol/L
- Creatinine 1.42 mg/dL (baseline 0.9 mg/dL), consistent with acute kidney injury
- Venous blood gas: pH 7.29; lactate 9.6 mmol/L
- Urinalysis: bilirubin, protein, leukocytes, and white blood cell casts present

Imaging: CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis shows an enlarged steatotic liver and mild fat stranding around the bladder and the appendix.
