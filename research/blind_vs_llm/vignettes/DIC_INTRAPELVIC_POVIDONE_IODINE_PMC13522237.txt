A 52-year-old woman is being managed during and immediately after elective urologic surgery.

Background: Genitourinary tuberculosis diagnosed by urine nucleic acid amplification testing, on four-drug antitubercular therapy for 3 months. Small atrophic left kidney with a solitary functioning right kidney drained by a right percutaneous nephrostomy. She underwent augmentation cystoplasty with right ureteric reimplantation for a contracted bladder. Piperacillin-tazobactam was given as perioperative prophylaxis.

Event: During the operation, dilute povidone-iodine was instilled antegradely through the nephrostomy under pressure to locate the ureteric orifice. Within 5-10 minutes of the instillation she developed rigors. Progressive diffuse oozing then appeared throughout the abdominal operative field, particularly from raw surfaces, requiring repeated hemostatic measures. Progressive hypotension developed intraoperatively and required vasopressor support; 2 units of packed red blood cells were transfused before transfer to the intensive care unit. After surgery her hemodynamic status continued to deteriorate despite fluid resuscitation and escalating vasopressor support (no numeric blood pressure reported).

Examination after surgery: generalized abdominal distension, persistent diffuse wound and drain-site bleeding, and extensive ecchymoses.

Initial postoperative laboratory results:
- Hemoglobin 6.6 g/dL (after 2 units of packed red cells)
- Platelets 91 x10^9/L
- INR 2.46
- aPTT 60.6 s
- Creatinine 1.57 mg/dL

Imaging: Ultrasonography showed no significant intra-abdominal collection.
