A 29-year-old woman (G5P3) with no known medical history was admitted from the emergency department. She reported a 3-month history of progressive rash, night sweats, chills, lymphadenopathy, dysphagia, headache and arthralgia, and new-onset diarrhea. Her sore throat (pharyngitis) had progressed to the point that she could not tolerate solids or liquids, and she was started on intravenous fluids for dehydration.

One week before this admission, at an emergency department visit, her CRP was 184 mg/L and ESR 85 mm/h.

On admission:
- Blood pressure 106/71 mmHg, pulse 108 bpm, temperature 37.4 °C, oxygen saturation 98%
- Skin: rash throughout the body, including the palms and soles; scattered solitary nodules (not grouped, not dermatomal) on the face, neck and back, and two on the right wrist
- Diffuse painful lymphadenopathy
- Arthralgia with generalized joint swelling and bilateral knee tenderness

Admission laboratory result: aPTT 44 s.
