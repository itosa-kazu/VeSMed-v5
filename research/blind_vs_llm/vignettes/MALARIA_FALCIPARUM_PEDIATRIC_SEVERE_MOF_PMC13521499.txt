A 5-year-old girl with a history of developmental delay presents with a 5-day history of fever, progressive fatigue, vomiting, diarrhea, and reduced oral intake, despite symptomatic treatment with ondansetron.

Exposure history: She returned from the Democratic Republic of the Congo 2 weeks ago. She did not take any chemoprophylaxis for the trip.

Examination at the first hospital: tachycardic, tachypneic, lethargic, clinically dehydrated, with diffuse abdominal tenderness (numeric vital signs not reported).

Laboratory results at the first hospital:
- White blood cells 17,400/uL; hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL; platelets 4,500/uL
- Blood urea nitrogen 76 mg/dL; creatinine 1.89 mg/dL (acute kidney injury); potassium 5.9 mmol/L
- Total bilirubin 6.9 mg/dL; AST 189 U/L; ALT 99 U/L
- Lactate 8 mmol/L

She is transferred the same day to a tertiary pediatric emergency department. On arrival she appears critically ill, with altered mental status, cool extremities, and a temperature of 103.5 °F (39.7 °C). She is tachycardic, with blood pressure appropriate for age. Head CT shows no cerebral edema.
