## Rankable Evidence Pulled

- Demographics: 33-year-old female.
- Risk context: history of hypertension.
- Symptoms and neurologic presentation: sudden severe holocephalic headache, neck pain, onset while bending over, photophobia, right lower extremity numbness, brief bilateral abnormal motor activity lasting 30 seconds, post-event fatigue, NIHSS 0, and pain worsened by neck flexion.
- Vital sign: blood pressure 169/75 mmHg.
- Initial CT: diffuse subarachnoid hemorrhage with reported cisternal, fissural, and cortical sulcal distribution.
- Initial vascular imaging: CTA head/neck and catheter DSA reported no aneurysm, stenosis, or dissection.
- MRI: focal susceptibility artifact in the right parafalcine frontal lobe, recorded as a neutral imaging observation.

## Record-Only Evidence

- Article title and abstract causal framing.

## Caveats

- No laboratory panel, temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, Glasgow Coma Scale score, trauma history, family history, or detailed medication history is reported.
- The source states that MRI was suspicious for an underlying lesion type; only the objective susceptibility artifact and location were used for ranking.
- The day-7 repeat angiography is objective, but it directly triggers the authors' conclusion, so it was placed in record-only rather than rankable observations.
